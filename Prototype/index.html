Technical Note: Methodology, Assumptions & Fallback

1. Linking Noisy GPS Points to Routes (Map-Matching): Raw GPS logs from bus_movements_YYYY_MM_DD.db exhibit urban drift and occasional dropouts. We reject velocity outliers (>80 km/h) and points >150m from the route polyline in shapes.txt, then project each valid ping orthogonally onto the directed stop-to-stop segment to compute monotonic distance-along-route $d(t)$.

2. Separating Cruise Time vs. Stop & Signal Waiting: Buses spend 15–35% of total journey time stationary at bus stops and signalized intersections (e.g., ITO, Ashram Chowk, Dhaula Kuan). Using a 40m stop proximity buffer, our SQLite historical profiler decomposes segment duration into road link cruise time and stop/signal dwell time.

3. Combining Live GTFS-RT & Historical Data (Sparse Fallback): Multiple bus routes share physical road corridors. We pool live segment traversals across all routes sharing edge $e$ in the past 20 minutes ($N_e$ vehicles) and blend with the historical $(e, \text{hour}, \text{day\_type})$ profile via Empirical Bayes shrinkage:
T_est(e,t) = [N_e / (N_e + 4)] * T_live(e,t) + [4 / (N_e + 4)] * T_hist(e,h,d) When a road has zero live buses at night ($N_e = 0 \Rightarrow \alpha = 0$), the estimator smoothly falls back to the historical time-of-day profile without instability.

4. Time-Dependent Routing & Quiet-Hour Traffic Comparison: Our RAPTOR-based journey planner advances the virtual clock at each stop along candidate direct and 1-transfer routes, and simultaneously evaluates the exact same path at 23:00 (11 PM Quiet Hour) to quantify traffic delay in minutes.
